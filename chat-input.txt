The water cycle describes how water moves between the ocean, the atmosphere,
and the land. Energy from the sun evaporates water from the ocean surface,
turning liquid water into vapour that rises into the air. Plants add to this
vapour through transpiration, releasing water drawn up by their roots.

As vapour rises it cools, and cooling reduces how much water the air can hold.
The vapour condenses onto small particles of dust and salt, forming the
droplets that make up clouds. When enough droplets merge, they grow heavy
enough to fall as rain, snow, or hail, depending on the temperature of the air
they pass through.

Water that reaches the ground either soaks into the soil or runs across the
surface into streams and rivers. Water in the soil may be taken up by plants,
or it may percolate deeper to join the groundwater, which moves slowly through
porous rock. Rivers and groundwater both return water to the ocean, where the
cycle begins again.
